City Medical Clinic — How Appointment Booking Works

Information required to book
To book an appointment the receptionist needs the patient's full name, a callback phone number, the reason for the visit, a preferred date, and a preferred time. New patients also need their date of birth. A booking is not final until the receptionist repeats the name, date, time, and reason and the patient confirms them.

Visit lengths
A standard follow-up or sick visit is 20 minutes. A new-patient visit or annual physical is 40 minutes. Well-child visits are 30 minutes. Only one concern is scheduled per sick visit. If you need a physical and a new problem addressed, book two separate appointments.

How available times are offered
The receptionist offers open times on the requested date. Morning appointments start at 8:00 AM, 8:30 AM, 9:00 AM, 9:30 AM, 10:00 AM, and 10:30 AM. Afternoon appointments start at 1:00 PM, 1:30 PM, 2:00 PM, 2:30 PM, 3:00 PM, 3:30 PM, and 4:00 PM. Saturday slots, when the clinic is open, are 9:00 AM, 10:00 AM, 11:00 AM, and 12:00 PM. If the requested time is taken, the receptionist offers the nearest open slot on that day before suggesting another date.

Same-day visits
A limited number of same-day sick slots open at 7:30 AM on weekdays. They are for new symptoms that started within the last few days. Same-day slots cannot be used for physicals, paperwork, or routine follow-up.

Confirmation
After the patient confirms the details, the receptionist saves the booking and gives a confirmation that includes a booking ID. A reminder call is placed one business day before the visit. Patients should call back if any detail in the confirmation is wrong.

Changing or canceling
Appointments can be moved or canceled up to 24 hours before the start time with no fee. Changes inside 24 hours follow the late-cancel policy in the clinic policies. To change a visit, the patient gives their name and the current date and time of the appointment.

What the receptionist cannot book
The receptionist does not book procedures that a clinician has not ordered, does not promise a specific clinician unless that clinician has an open slot, and does not book visits for problems that belong in the emergency department. Those callers are told to call 911.
